Solution areas

The problems these systems actually solve

Organisations do not buy technology; they buy the removal of a specific operational problem. These are the problems we are asked to solve most often.

Process Digitisation

A process that currently lives in registers, printed forms or a shared spreadsheet becomes a system: defined roles, defined states, a record of who did what and when, and data you can report on.

Workflow Automation

Repetitive coordination — routing, reminders, escalations, status updates, handovers — is executed by software on a schedule or a trigger, instead of people chasing each other.

Digital Service Platforms

A portal through which an organisation delivers a service to the people who use it: they submit, they track, they receive; your team processes the queue in a structured back office.

Monitoring, MIS & Reporting

The numbers a decision needs are in one place and refresh themselves, instead of being assembled from several systems each time someone asks.

Document & Data Automation

Information is extracted, classified, validated and routed out of documents and records at volume, with low-confidence cases sent to a person rather than guessed at.

Communication Automation

Structured, template-based communication over web, email, WhatsApp and SMS — sent on rules, logged, and with opt-out and consent handling built in.

System Integration

Existing systems, databases and third-party platforms are connected so that data is entered once and moves reliably, with failures visible rather than silent.

Security & reliability

Designed with access control, traceability and recovery in mind

Security is part of the architecture stage, not a review at the end. We are specific about the practices we follow — and equally specific about the certifications we do not hold.

Access control

Role-based permissions with least-privilege defaults, authentication and session handling, and a documented model of who can see and change what.

Auditability

Privileged and data-changing actions are logged with actor, timestamp and outcome, so an action can be reconstructed months later.

Secure engineering

Input validation, output encoding, parameterised queries, encrypted secrets, TLS in transit, and dependency updates as routine maintenance.

Recovery

Scheduled backups with restores actually tested, environment separation, and a documented rollback path for every deployment.

Monitoring

Uptime, error and resource monitoring with alerting, so failures are detected by the system rather than reported by a user.

Human control

Consequential actions are drafted by the system and confirmed by a named person. Controls are removed deliberately, per workflow, not assumed away.

Why Avokara

What is different about working with us

Six specific things, each of which has a practical consequence for the organisation buying the work.

One team from requirement to support

The people who map your workflow also design the architecture, write the code, run the deployment and answer the support request. Nothing is lost in a handover between a consulting layer and a delivery layer.

AI where it earns its place, engineering everywhere else

We use language models for work that genuinely needs reading and judgement, and ordinary deterministic software for everything else. A rule that must always hold is written as a rule, not asked of a model.

API-first and integration-ready by default

Every system we build exposes and consumes documented interfaces, on the assumption that something else will need to talk to it later — because it usually does.

Built to be handed over

Standard open technologies, source code in version control you own, written architecture and admin documentation. If you move the work in-house or to another vendor, nothing has to be reverse-engineered.

Approval gates and audit trails as a design default

Consequential actions are drafted by the system and confirmed by a named person, and privileged actions are logged. Controls are removed deliberately, per workflow, once you are confident — never assumed away at the start.

A plan for year three, not just launch week

Monitoring, backups, dependency updates, performance work and an agreed change process are quoted as part of the engagement, not discovered afterwards.

Common questions

What does Avokara do?

We are a technology company. We design, build, integrate and maintain software: custom web applications and portals, AI-driven automation, API integrations between existing systems, operational dashboards and MIS, and the cloud infrastructure they run on. We take a project from requirement discovery through architecture, development, integration, testing, deployment and training, and we support it afterwards. See services and engineering capabilities.

What size and type of organisation do you work with?

Organisations that have a workflow complex enough to need software built around it — institutions, public-service bodies, educational and healthcare organisations, financial and professional services firms, enterprises, and growing businesses. The common factor is a process with multiple roles, approvals, records and reporting obligations, not a particular headcount.

Can you handle integrations with systems we already run?

Yes — that is a large part of what we do. We design and consume REST APIs, build middleware and scheduled synchronisation jobs, and integrate identity, payment, messaging and third-party platforms. Where a system has no API, we work with the interfaces it does expose (database, file exchange, email) and are explicit about the limits of that approach. See API and system integration.

How do you handle security and access control?

As part of the design, not a review at the end: role-based access control with least-privilege defaults, authentication and session handling, input validation, encrypted secrets, TLS in transit, audit logging of privileged actions, and tested backups. We describe our practices in detail on the security and reliability page, and we do not claim certifications we do not hold.

Do you support and maintain systems after they go live?

Yes. Monitoring, fixes, dependency and security updates, performance work and agreed enhancements are part of how we scope an engagement rather than an afterthought. If you would rather maintain the system internally or move it to another vendor, you get the source code, documentation and a handover — we build on standard open technologies for that reason. See support and maintenance.

How does a project start?

With a requirement discussion, not a quote. We work through the objective, the workflow as it is actually performed, the systems already in place and the constraints, then propose a scope, architecture approach and timeline in writing — including what is deliberately out of scope. Describe your requirement to begin.

Have a system to build, replace or integrate?

Describe the requirement and the workflow behind it. We will tell you what we would build, how we would architect it, and what we would not recommend — in writing, before any commitment.
