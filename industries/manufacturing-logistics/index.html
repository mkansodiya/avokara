AI automation for Manufacturing & Logistics

In manufacturing and distribution, most communication is status: order received, in production, dispatched, delivered, payment due. Automating these updates — plus chasing vendor POs and flagging exceptions like delayed dispatches — removes a large clerical load and reduces the “where is my order” calls that interrupt your team.

Problems we see most often

  • Customers calling repeatedly for order and dispatch status
  • Vendor purchase orders followed up ad hoc, causing material delays
  • Exceptions (delays, short supplies, quality holds) surfacing late
  • Daily reporting assembled manually from spreadsheets

Automations that pay back fastest

Order lifecycle updates

Automatic acknowledgement, production, and dispatch notifications by email or WhatsApp at each stage.

Vendor PO follow-ups

Open POs chased on schedule with escalation when confirmations don't arrive.

Exception digests

A daily email flagging delayed orders, pending approvals, and stuck shipments before customers notice.

Report assembly

Dispatch, pending-order, and receivables summaries compiled automatically each morning.

Tools we commonly connect

Tally (via connector), Zoho Books/Inventory, Google Sheets, Gmail, WhatsApp Business, transporter tracking emails.

Frequently asked questions

Our data lives in Tally and Excel — is that workable?

Yes. Tally connects via its XML interface or scheduled exports, and structured Excel/Sheets work directly. We often start from the sheets you already maintain rather than forcing a new system.

Our processes are on the factory floor, not in software — what can automation do?

We automate the information flow around the physical process: status capture can be as simple as a supervisor updating one sheet or sending one WhatsApp message, and the system handles all downstream communication from there.

How disruptive is implementation?

Minimal by design — automations sit alongside your existing tools rather than replacing them. Typical builds go live in 2–4 weeks with no downtime to operations.
