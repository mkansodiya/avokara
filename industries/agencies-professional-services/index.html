AI automation for Agencies & Professional Services

Agencies and professional-services firms sell time, so admin work directly eats margin. The automations that pay back fastest: qualifying and replying to inbound leads the same day, chasing sent proposals on schedule, assembling client status reports from your project tools, and running invoice reminders. Each one replaces a recurring block of partner or account-manager time.

Problems we see most often

  • Inbound leads answered days late because everyone is busy with client work
  • Proposals sent and then forgotten until the prospect goes cold
  • Weekly client reporting that takes hours of copy-paste from project tools
  • Invoices chased awkwardly and irregularly by the same person who manages the relationship

Automations that pay back fastest

Lead qualification and first response

AI scores enquiries against your ideal client profile and drafts a personalised reply for approval — sent same-day.

Proposal follow-up sequences

Every sent proposal gets tracked and chased at set intervals until there's an answer.

Client report assembly

Status data pulled from your PM tool and drafted into a client-ready weekly email.

Invoice reminders

Polite escalating reminders that separate the money conversation from the relationship.

Tools we commonly connect

Gmail/Google Workspace, Notion, Asana, Trello, ClickUp, Zoho, HubSpot, Google Sheets, Slack.

Frequently asked questions

Our leads are referrals and each one is different — can automation still help?

Yes. Referral leads still benefit from a fast, personalised first response and disciplined follow-up; the AI drafts from context (who referred, what they asked) and you approve. The judgement stays with you; the discipline comes from the system.

Can client reports really be automated?

The assembly can: pulling task status, hours, and milestones from your project tool into a drafted email. The account manager reviews, adds judgement, and sends. Teams typically go from hours to minutes per report.

We're only a 5-person team — is this overkill?

Small teams often benefit most, because there's no ops person to absorb the admin. A starter build (lead response + proposal chasing) is deliberately scoped small — see engagement models.
