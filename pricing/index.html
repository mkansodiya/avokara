Engagement models

How engagements are structured and priced

Avokara works on fixed-scope, fixed-price projects quoted in writing after requirement discovery, with larger scopes sequenced into separately-quoted releases, and optional ongoing support. Indicative ranges for the scopes we quote most often: single workflows and integrations from ₹15,000; departmental systems from ₹60,000; multi-function systems from ₹1,50,000; support and maintenance from ₹12,000 per month. Programme-scale work is quoted individually. GST additional where applicable.

Four ways to engage us

Most work is the first of these. The others exist because not every requirement is a full build.

Most common

Fixed-scope project

A defined system, quoted in writing after requirement discovery: scope, deliverables, stage plan, timeline, payment stages and explicit exclusions. Suits a build with a requirement that can be pinned down.

  • All eight delivery stages, each with a reviewable output
  • Fixed price against a fixed, written scope
  • Defect correction against scope included in delivery
  • Documentation, training and handover
Large scope

Staged programme

A large scope architected as a whole and delivered as a sequence of releases, each with its own scope, price and sign-off, so a working part reaches production early and cost is committed incrementally.

  • Architecture designed for the full scope from the start
  • Each release scoped, quoted and accepted separately
  • Value in production before the programme completes
  • Sequence reviewable between releases
Ongoing

Support & maintenance

Monthly arrangement after go-live: monitoring, defect fixes, security and dependency updates, performance work, backup verification and an agreed allowance for small changes.

  • Written response expectations by severity
  • Single tracked channel for issues and requests
  • Change process for anything larger than the allowance
  • Available for systems built elsewhere, after assessment

See support and maintenance.

Alongside your team

Defined technical scope

A specific piece of engineering delivered alongside your existing team or vendor — an API layer, an integration, a dashboard, a data migration, or a technical review of an existing system.

  • Scoped and quoted as a discrete deliverable
  • Works with your existing architecture and constraints
  • Written findings where the scope is a review
  • No requirement to replace what already works

What moves a project within a range

  • Number of roles and the complexity of the permission model. Three roles with clean boundaries is straightforward; eleven roles with delegation, regional scoping and maker–checker approvals is a substantial part of the build.
  • Number and quality of integrations. Each external system adds design, build, test and failure-handling work. A system with a documented API costs a fraction of one without.
  • Condition of existing data. Clean, complete records migrate cheaply. Inconsistent historical data needs assessment, cleanup and reconciliation, quoted as its own item.
  • How much judgement the system has to exercise. Notifying someone is simple; deciding something, with edge cases and an audit trail behind the decision, is not.
  • Reporting depth. A few operational views is not the same as a consolidated MIS across several sources with role-scoped distribution.
  • Compliance and operational surface. Approved messaging templates and consent handling, audit-log requirements, data-residency constraints, penetration testing by a third party, or a formal acceptance process all add real, quotable work.

Running costs after go-live

Estimated for your specific case before you commit, and generally three lines:

  • Infrastructure. Nothing additional if it runs on servers you already have; otherwise a cloud or VPS cost sized to expected volume.
  • Third-party services. Language-model APIs, messaging, payment or identity providers, billed on usage by those providers. Where an AI step is involved, we size it against your expected volume rather than quoting a generic figure.
  • Support and maintenance. Optional, and quoted as part of the original engagement so the cost of owning the system is known before it is built.

Procurement and documentation

We are happy to work to your organisation's own engagement format — your agreement template, purchase-order process, milestone certification, invoicing requirements, NDA, security questionnaire or vendor-onboarding documentation. Company registration documents are available on request, and the company capability profile is formatted to print or save as a PDF for an evaluation file.

Where an engagement requires one we do not hold, we will tell you during scoping rather than let it surface later.

Commercial questions

Why are these ranges rather than prices?

Because the same description covers very different amounts of work. A workflow system touching one database and one email account is not the same build as one integrating three external systems, handling document verification and reporting to four roles. The range tells you the order of magnitude honestly; the written quote after discovery tells you the number.

What is the cost of a larger programme?

Quoted individually after discovery, and sequenced into releases each with its own scope and price, so you are never committing to a single large undisclosed figure. We do not publish an indicative range for programme-scale work because we would be guessing, and a guess on this page is worth nothing to you.

What are the ongoing costs after a build?

Three lines, all estimated before you commit: infrastructure (your own servers, your cloud account, or a managed environment we arrange); third-party services the system depends on, such as language-model APIs, messaging or payment providers, billed on usage; and support and maintenance if you want us to keep operating it.

How are payments staged?

Against delivery stages rather than dates — typically on completion of discovery and architecture, on prototype sign-off, on staging delivery, and on production acceptance. The exact schedule is part of the written engagement. We are happy to work to your organisation's own payment, purchase-order or milestone-certification process.

Do you charge for the requirement discussion?

No. The initial requirement discussion is free and carries no obligation. Where a requirement needs substantial paid discovery before it can be scoped at all — for example mapping an undocumented process across several units — we will say so and quote that stage separately rather than absorb it into a fixed price that then slips.

What happens if the delivered system does not do what was agreed?

The scope document states what the system will do and what it will not, so 'working' is defined in writing before the build rather than argued about after it. Defects against the agreed scope are fixed as part of delivery, not billed as changes.
