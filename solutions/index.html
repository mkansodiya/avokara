Solution areas

Solution areas

Organised by the operational problem being solved rather than by the technology used to solve it. Most engagements combine two or three of these.

Avokara works across seven solution areas: process digitisation, workflow automation, digital service platforms, monitoring and MIS, document and data automation, communication automation, and system integration. Each is a class of operational problem — work that is manual, repeated, invisible, or spread across systems that do not talk to each other.

Process Digitisation

A process that currently lives in registers, printed forms or a shared spreadsheet becomes a system: defined roles, defined states, a record of who did what and when, and data you can report on.

What this typically includes
  • Application and request intake replacing paper or email forms
  • Multi-step approval chains with delegation and escalation
  • Records, attachments and status history in one place
  • Exportable registers and audit trails

Workflow Automation

Repetitive coordination — routing, reminders, escalations, status updates, handovers — is executed by software on a schedule or a trigger, instead of people chasing each other.

What this typically includes
  • Rule-based routing and assignment
  • Deadline, SLA and reminder handling
  • Exception detection and alerting
  • Human-in-the-loop approval before consequential actions

Digital Service Platforms

A portal through which an organisation delivers a service to the people who use it: they submit, they track, they receive; your team processes the queue in a structured back office.

What this typically includes
  • Public-facing submission and status tracking
  • Authenticated user accounts and role-based back office
  • Document upload, verification and issue
  • Notification and acknowledgement trails

Monitoring, MIS & Reporting

The numbers a decision needs are in one place and refresh themselves, instead of being assembled from several systems each time someone asks.

What this typically includes
  • Operational dashboards by role and region
  • Scheduled reports delivered by email
  • Consolidation of data from multiple systems
  • Threshold alerts and trend views

Document & Data Automation

Information is extracted, classified, validated and routed out of documents and records at volume, with low-confidence cases sent to a person rather than guessed at.

What this typically includes
  • Extraction from PDFs, scans, forms and email
  • Classification and routing by content
  • Validation rules and confidence thresholds
  • Structured output into a database or existing system

Communication Automation

Structured, template-based communication over web, email, WhatsApp and SMS — sent on rules, logged, and with opt-out and consent handling built in.

What this typically includes
  • Acknowledgements, reminders and status notifications
  • Approved templates and versioning
  • Delivery logging and failure handling
  • Consent and opt-out records

System Integration

Existing systems, databases and third-party platforms are connected so that data is entered once and moves reliably, with failures visible rather than silent.

What this typically includes
  • REST API design and consumption
  • Middleware and data synchronisation jobs
  • Identity, payment and messaging provider integration
  • Retry, idempotency and reconciliation handling
Recognising the problem

Signals that a process needs a system

If several of these describe an operation you are responsible for, the underlying issue is usually structural rather than a matter of effort.

The same data is entered more than once

A record is keyed into one system, copied into a spreadsheet, then re-typed into a report. Each copy is an opportunity for the three versions to disagree, and reconciliation becomes a recurring job.

Status is a question, not a field

Finding out where something stands requires asking a person. Nothing is wrong with the process except that its state is not recorded anywhere a system can read.

Reporting is assembled by hand

A monthly or weekly report takes someone days to compile from several sources, which means it is always describing the past and can never be checked mid-period.

Approvals move by email and memory

Who approved what, when and on what basis is reconstructed from inboxes. Delegation during absence is informal, and nothing enforces sequence.

Exceptions are found late

Overdue items, stalled cases and failures surface when someone happens to look, or when the affected person complains, rather than when they occur.

Institutional knowledge sits with individuals

One or two people know how the process really runs. Absence or departure is an operational risk because the rules are not written anywhere a system enforces.

The engineering behind these

Questions about scoping a solution

We know the problem but not the solution. Is that a problem?

No — it is the normal starting point and the reason the first delivery stage is requirement discovery rather than quoting. Describe the process as it is performed today, including the parts that are worked around informally, and we will propose what to build.

Is it better to fix the process first or automate it?

Fix it first. Automating a process nobody agrees on multiplies the disagreement. Part of discovery is identifying steps that exist only because of an old system constraint — those should be removed, not rebuilt faithfully in software.

Can a large process be delivered in stages?

Yes, and for anything substantial we recommend it. One workflow in production, being used by real people, teaches more than a full specification does — and it means value arrives before the whole programme is finished. The architecture is designed for the full scope; the delivery is sequenced.

What if some of our data is in poor condition?

Very common, and better dealt with explicitly. We assess data quality during discovery and quote cleanup and migration as their own scope item, rather than discovering mid-build that the records cannot support the reporting that was asked for.

Describe the process, not the software

Tell us how the work is performed today, where it stalls and who has to sign off on what. That is enough for us to propose an approach and a sequence.
