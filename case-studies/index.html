What a client case study will contain

When client work is published here, it will follow the same structure — because that is what a technical evaluator actually needs:

  • Challenge — the operational problem, stated concretely
  • Solution — what was built, and what was deliberately not built
  • Architecture — how the system is structured and what it integrates with
  • Technology — the actual stack used
  • Outcome — measured results where they are genuinely known and the client permits publication

Where an outcome cannot be measured or shared, we will say so rather than substitute a plausible number.

How to evaluate us

What to look at when there is no long client list

A young company cannot show you fifteen years of references, and pretending otherwise would be the first thing to distrust. What you can assess instead is whether the engineering thinking holds up:

  • Read the internal case study, including the failures. The four problems documented there — a broken approval return path, a cartesian join in a merge step, generic AI imagery, and collapsed line breaks in an email template — are the kind of detail only someone who actually built the system can describe.
  • Read the honest limitations on every service page. They tell you where we would decline work, which is more informative than a capability list.
  • Read the security page, particularly the certifications section. We state what we do not hold. Compare that with vendors who imply what they do not have.
  • Ask for a written architecture note before committing. We produce one at stage two of every project. Judge the thinking in it.
  • Start with a contained scope. One workflow, one integration, one dashboard. It is the cheapest way to find out whether a supplier can execute, and we would rather earn a larger engagement than be handed one.
