Running Avokara on 11 n8n workflows

By Nitin Singh · Published 2 August 2026 · Every claim on this page is from our own production system.

Summary: Before offering automation to clients, we automated Avokara itself: 11 production n8n workflows now handle our lead pipeline, sales follow-ups, support triage, invoice reminders, HR screening, daily operations digest, and a LinkedIn content pipeline with AI-generated branded graphics. Total infrastructure cost: a self-hosted n8n instance (server-cost only) plus roughly ₹500–₹1,500/month in AI API usage. Build time: about two weeks of focused work, including the mistakes documented below.

The problem

A new agency has the same operational needs as an established one — leads must be answered, invoices chased, content published, operations watched — but no staff to do it. The founder's time was going into repetitive coordination instead of client work. We decided the company's own operations would be the first client project, built to the same standard we'd sell.

The solution: architecture

The system is 11 n8n workflows on a self-hosted Docker instance, organised around three shared services and eight function agents:

WorkflowWhat it doesHuman gate?
Error handlerCatches any workflow failure, emails an alert with details—
Approval gateShared sub-workflow: emails a draft with approve/reject buttons, waits up to 48hIs the gate
Daily digestOne 9 AM email: new leads, stuck items, overdue invoices, ops notesRead-only
Lead generation agentCaptures and enriches enquiries into the pipeline—
Sales agentQualifies leads, drafts personalised follow-ups, tracks proposals, writes status backYes — every send
Marketing agentDrafts weekly social posts and newsletter as one approval batchYes
Support agentTriage + drafted replies for incoming support emailYes
Finance agentInvoice reminders at 7/15/30 days overdue, escalating toneYes — every reminder
HR agentScreens applications against role criteria, drafts responsesYes
Operations agent8:30 AM sweep: flags stuck leads, old tickets, stale approvals into the digestRead-only
LinkedIn trend agentMon/Wed/Fri: reads AI-industry news feeds, writes a founder-voice post, generates a branded graphic (logo and URL stamped programmatically, not by AI), emails a ready-to-post packageYes — founder posts manually

Implementation details that mattered

  • Write-backs prevent repeats. Early versions could email the same lead twice because status lived only in the workflow's head. Every action now writes state back to the pipeline sheet before anything sends.
  • The approval gate is a shared sub-workflow, not copy-pasted logic — one place to fix, consistent behaviour everywhere.
  • Brand elements are stamped, not generated. AI image models misspell text unpredictably; our logo and domain are composited onto each generated graphic programmatically, so brand text is pixel-perfect every time.
  • Everything logs to one activity sheet, which is what makes the daily digest and stuck-item detection possible.

What went wrong (and the fixes)

  • Email approvals initially returned the wrong data downstream, losing the approval decision — fixed by restructuring the gate's return path.
  • A merge step produced a cartesian join that multiplied digest rows — fixed by switching merge strategy and classifying rows by field shape.
  • AI-generated images looked generic until we split art direction (varies per post) from brand frame (fixed), and moved brand text out of the AI's hands entirely.
  • Platform email formatting collapsed line breaks in one client-facing template — caught in testing, fixed by switching to explicit HTML email bodies.

Measurable results

  • 11 workflows in production, covering seven business functions, built in roughly two weeks.
  • Running cost: self-hosted n8n (server cost only) + AI usage currently in the ₹500–₹1,500/month range at our volume, dominated by image generation (about ₹22 per high-quality branded graphic at current OpenAI pricing).
  • Daily routine: operations oversight is one digest email and a handful of one-click approvals — minutes, not hours.
  • Zero unattended customer-facing sends: every outbound email and post passes a human gate, by design.

What we'd tell a client from this build

Start with the digest and one agent, not all eleven. Put approval gates everywhere first and remove them selectively later. Budget real time for testing against live traffic — half our fixes came from real data, not planning. And treat brand assets as code, not as something to hope the AI renders correctly.
