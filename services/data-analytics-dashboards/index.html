Data, Dashboards & MIS

An MIS or operational dashboard is what replaces the recurring job of assembling a report by hand. Avokara builds reporting systems that consolidate data from the systems you already run, present it by role and by unit, refresh on a schedule, and raise an alert when a threshold is crossed — so a number can be checked mid-period rather than compiled after it.

What is included

  • Reporting requirement analysis: which decisions the numbers support, and who is accountable for each
  • Data source assessment across existing systems, including quality, completeness and update frequency
  • Data model for reporting: aggregation levels, time periods, and reporting views that do not slow the source
  • Scheduled extraction and consolidation from multiple systems into a single reporting store
  • Role-based dashboards so each user sees their own unit, region or portfolio
  • Operational views: queues, ageing, stalled items, exceptions and workload distribution
  • Trend and comparison views over defined periods
  • Threshold alerts delivered by email or message when a metric crosses a defined limit
  • Scheduled report distribution in the formats recipients actually use
  • Exports and print layouts for records and reports that must leave the system

Typical engagements

Shapes of work we are most often asked for in this area. Most engagements combine one of these with integration, reporting and support.

Consolidated operational dashboard

Data from several systems and spreadsheets is brought together into one refreshed view: current state, ageing, exceptions and workload, broken down by unit and role, replacing a report that previously took days to assemble.

Automated periodic reporting

A recurring report — weekly, monthly, or for an external obligation — is generated on schedule from source systems and delivered to its recipients in the required format, with the figures traceable back to the records behind them.

Monitoring and exception dashboard

Live operational state with thresholds: what is overdue, what has stalled, what has failed, what is trending the wrong way — with alerts so the exception finds the person rather than the person finding the exception.

Technical approach

A dashboard is only as trustworthy as the answer to one question: where did this number come from? That shapes how we build:

  • Every figure is traceable to its records. A user who does not believe a number needs to be able to drill into what produced it. A figure nobody can verify gets ignored, and then the manual report comes back.
  • Reporting does not run against the live application. Aggregation happens in a separate reporting path on a schedule, so a heavy report cannot slow down the system people are working in.
  • Definitions are written down. "Pending", "closed" and "active" mean different things to different units. Each metric gets a written definition, agreed before it is built, because disagreement about definitions is what discredits a dashboard.
  • Freshness is stated on screen. Every view shows when it was last refreshed. Users should never have to guess whether they are looking at today or yesterday.
  • Access is scoped like the source. A report inherits the same role restrictions as the screens the data came from, exports included.

Where source data is not good enough to support the reporting asked for, we say so during assessment and quote the cleanup as its own scope item, rather than building a dashboard that presents unreliable numbers convincingly.

Delivery and commercials

Work follows the eight-stage delivery process — requirement discovery, solution architecture, UI/UX and prototype, development, integration and testing, deployment, training and documentation, and maintenance and support — with each stage producing an output you can review and keep. Scope, timeline and cost are committed in writing after discovery, not before it. Engagement structures and indicative ranges are on the engagement models page.

Honest limitations

Things worth knowing before you engage us for this, rather than after:

  • A dashboard cannot improve data it is given. Where source records are incomplete or inconsistently entered, the honest first step is fixing capture and cleaning history — which we quote separately rather than hiding inside a reporting project.
  • Real-time and near-real-time have very different costs. Most operational reporting does not need sub-minute freshness, and we will ask what decision depends on the difference before designing for it.
  • Where metric definitions are contested between units, that has to be resolved before the build. We can facilitate it, but we cannot decide it for you.
  • We build reporting into applications and as standalone reporting systems. Where you have standardised on a specific commercial BI platform, we will integrate with it or tell you plainly if supporting it well is outside what we deliver today.

Frequently asked questions

Can you pull data from systems we did not build?

Usually, yes — via an API, a read-only database connection, a scheduled export, or a file exchange. Assessment establishes what each source can provide and how often, which is what determines achievable freshness. See API and system integration.

Who can see which numbers?

Whatever your access model requires. Role-based scoping is built in, so a district officer sees their district, a unit head sees their unit, and a central role sees the consolidated view — including in exports and scheduled reports, which is where access control is most often forgotten.

Do reports have to be opened in the dashboard?

No. Scheduled reports can be generated and delivered by email or message in the format recipients actually use. For many senior users a report that arrives on time is more useful than a dashboard they have to remember to log into — we build both.

Can we get alerts instead of having to check?

Yes. Thresholds are defined per metric, and crossing one sends a notification to the responsible role. This is usually the highest-value part of a reporting project: the exception finds the person rather than waiting to be noticed.
