Cloud, Infrastructure & DevOps

This is the work of getting an application running somewhere dependable and keeping it there: containerised deployment, backend and database architecture, separated environments, TLS, monitoring with alerting, scheduled backups with tested restores, and hardening. Avokara does this for systems we build and, where the scope is clear, for systems we did not.

Discuss a Requirement Engineering capabilities

What is included

  • Deployment architecture: what runs where, how it scales, and what the failure modes are
  • Containerised builds so the artefact that was tested is the artefact that is deployed
  • Environment separation: development, staging and production, with no testing against live data
  • Reverse proxy configuration, TLS certificates with automatic renewal, and security headers
  • Database architecture: schema, indexing, connection handling, and a migration process
  • Scheduled automated backups stored away from the running system, with restores tested
  • Uptime, error-rate and resource monitoring with alerting to a named channel
  • Log aggregation and retention sufficient to investigate an incident
  • Operating-system and dependency patching as scheduled maintenance
  • Deployment runbook, documented rollback path, and handover documentation

Typical engagements

Shapes of work we are most often asked for in this area. Most engagements combine one of these with integration, reporting and support.

Deployment of a system we built

The application is deployed into the required environment — your server, your cloud account, or infrastructure we manage — with TLS, separated environments, monitoring, backups and a documented rollback path, and handed over with a runbook.

Hardening and monitoring an existing deployment

A system already in production but running without monitoring, backups, TLS discipline or environment separation is assessed and brought to a defensible standard, with the gaps and the risks stated in writing first.

Migration to a different environment

Moving an application between servers, providers or from a managed service to infrastructure you control, with a data migration plan, reconciliation, a rehearsed cutover and a rollback path.

Technical approach

Infrastructure work is judged by what happens on a bad day, so that is what we design for:

  • Deployment is scripted, not remembered. A release should be repeatable by someone who was not there last time, which is also what makes a rollback possible under pressure.
  • Nothing reaches production without passing staging. Staging exists so that the deployment itself is tested, not only the code.
  • Backups are only real once a restore has been performed. An untested backup is a hope. Restores are tested as part of handover and periodically afterwards.
  • Monitoring should tell you before a user does. Uptime, error rate and resource pressure alert to a named channel; a central error handler raises failures rather than swallowing them.
  • Prefer boring, well-understood infrastructure. A simple architecture the client's own team can understand and operate is worth more than a sophisticated one that only we can run.

Where data must remain on infrastructure you control, that is a design input from the first stage, not a later change. Containerised deployment is what makes the same build portable across your environment and ours.

Delivery and commercials

Work follows the eight-stage delivery process — requirement discovery, solution architecture, UI/UX and prototype, development, integration and testing, deployment, training and documentation, and maintenance and support — with each stage producing an output you can review and keep. Scope, timeline and cost are committed in writing after discovery, not before it. Engagement structures and indicative ranges are on the engagement models page.

Honest limitations

Things worth knowing before you engage us for this, rather than after:

  • We are a software engineering company that operates what it builds competently. We are not a 24x7 network operations centre, and we do not present ourselves as one; support response expectations are agreed in writing rather than implied.
  • Taking on an existing deployment requires an assessment first, and sometimes the honest finding is that it should be rebuilt rather than patched. We will say that, with reasons, before taking payment for maintenance on something we cannot make dependable.
  • Scaling architecture is designed for your realistic expected volume plus headroom. Designing for hypothetical extreme scale costs money at every stage and is usually the wrong trade for the organisations we work with — we will tell you which case you are in.
  • Cloud provider and infrastructure costs are yours and are billed by the provider, not by us. We estimate them before you commit and design to keep them predictable.

Frequently asked questions

Can everything stay on our own servers?

Yes. Containerised deployment means the same tested build runs on your hardware, in your cloud account, or on infrastructure we manage. Where data residency or policy requires it, that is decided at the architecture stage.

Do you manage the infrastructure long term, or hand it over?

Either. Under a support arrangement we monitor, patch and deploy. Under handover you receive the runbook, the deployment configuration and the documentation needed for your team to operate it. Both are intended to be genuinely workable — nothing is configured in a way only we can understand.

What monitoring and alerting do we actually get?

Uptime checks, error-rate and resource monitoring, alerting to a channel you nominate, central error handling in the application, and retained logs. The specifics are agreed during architecture so you know what is watched and who is told.

How are backups handled?

Scheduled automated database backups, stored separately from the running system, retained to an agreed schedule, with restores tested rather than assumed. Recovery expectations are written down during architecture rather than discussed after an incident.
