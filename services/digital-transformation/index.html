Digital Transformation & Process Digitisation

Process digitisation is the work of turning a process that lives in registers, printed forms, email threads and shared spreadsheets into a system: defined roles, defined states, a record of who did what and when, and data that can be reported on. Avokara does this as an engineering project with a documented process map, not as a software purchase.

Discuss a Requirement Engineering capabilities

What is included

  • Current-state mapping: the process as actually performed, including the informal workarounds
  • Identification of steps that exist only because of an old constraint and should be removed, not rebuilt
  • Target-state design: roles, states, transitions, approvals, records and reporting requirements
  • Data assessment and a migration plan for existing records, including cleanup where needed
  • Digital intake replacing paper or email forms, with validation at the point of entry
  • Approval chains with delegation, sequence enforcement and escalation on delay
  • Document handling: upload, verification, versioning, and issue of generated documents
  • Notification and acknowledgement trails so people know their request was received and where it stands
  • Operational dashboards and exportable registers over the digitised process
  • Phased rollout, training for each role, and documentation written for a new joiner

Typical engagements

Shapes of work we are most often asked for in this area. Most engagements combine one of these with integration, reporting and support.

Paper or register process to digital workflow

An intake handled today on printed forms, with a physical register and approvals moving on file notes, becomes a digital queue: submitted online or entered at a counter, validated, routed, approved in sequence, recorded, and reportable at any moment.

Spreadsheet-run operation to a proper system

A process running on several shared spreadsheets — with version conflicts, no access control and no history — becomes an application with roles, validation, an audit trail and reporting, with the existing data migrated and cleaned.

Legacy process modernisation

An existing system that still works but no longer fits: we keep what is sound, rebuild what is not, integrate rather than replace where possible, and migrate data with reconciliation — rather than a single high-risk cutover.

Technical approach

Digitisation projects fail for predictable reasons. These are the ones we plan against:

  • Do not automate a process nobody agrees on. Where two units describe the same process differently, that disagreement has to be resolved before it is encoded in software, because software will make it visible and permanent. Part of discovery is surfacing exactly this.
  • Do not rebuild constraints faithfully. Many steps exist because a previous system, form layout or physical file required them. Carrying them into a new system preserves cost that could have been removed.
  • Data condition sets the ceiling on reporting. If the existing records are incomplete or inconsistent, no dashboard fixes that. We assess data quality during discovery and quote cleanup and migration as their own scope item.
  • Run the old and new process together at first. For anything operationally important, a parallel period with reconciliation is cheaper than a hard cutover, and it is how you find the cases the specification missed.
  • Adoption is the deliverable, not the launch. Rollout is phased by unit or region, each role is trained, and documentation is written so a new staff member can be brought up to speed without us.

Delivery and commercials

Work follows the eight-stage delivery process — requirement discovery, solution architecture, UI/UX and prototype, development, integration and testing, deployment, training and documentation, and maintenance and support — with each stage producing an output you can review and keep. Scope, timeline and cost are committed in writing after discovery, not before it. Engagement structures and indicative ranges are on the engagement models page.

Honest limitations

Things worth knowing before you engage us for this, rather than after:

  • This is organisational work as much as engineering. It needs a named decision-maker on your side who can settle process questions; projects stall over decision availability far more often than over code.
  • Not every process should be digitised. Some are low-volume enough that the system costs more than the work it saves. We will say so during discovery rather than build it.
  • Migration of poor-quality historical data has a real cost and sometimes an unavoidable loss of fidelity. We quantify that before the build, not after.
  • A phased rollout means running two ways of working for a period. That is a genuine operational burden, and it is the cheaper option compared with a failed cutover.

Frequently asked questions

Where should we start if the whole operation needs work?

With the process where the pain is measurable and the scope is containable — usually intake or approvals, because that is where delay accumulates and where a result is visible quickly. We architect for the full scope and deliver in that order, so the first release is genuinely useful rather than a pilot that gets abandoned.

What happens to our existing records?

They are assessed, mapped, cleaned where needed and migrated, with reconciliation against the source so you can verify nothing was lost. Where historical data is too inconsistent to migrate usefully, we say so and propose keeping it accessible as an archive rather than importing unreliable records into a new system.

Our staff are not comfortable with software. Is that a blocker?

No, but it is a design input. Interfaces are built for the actual users rather than for a demo: fewer screens, clearer language, validation that explains itself, and the option of assisted entry at a counter where the public cannot be expected to self-serve. Training is a delivery stage.

Can this work where connectivity is unreliable?

To a degree, and it changes the design. Interfaces are built light so they work on slow connections and mid-range devices, forms tolerate interruption, and where field capture genuinely happens offline we design for capture-then-sync with conflict handling. We are explicit about what is and is not possible offline rather than promising seamlessness.
